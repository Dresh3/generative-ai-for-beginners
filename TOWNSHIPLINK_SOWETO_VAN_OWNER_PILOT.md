## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**TownshipLink** begins with a **single high-density township pilot** (Soweto or Khayelitsha) rather than multi-market expansion. This focused approach validates unit economics, builds Zone Captain network, and **partners with local van owners for last-mile delivery**—creating a complementary revenue stream and deeper community integration.

### Revised Investment Thesis (Township-First)
- **Pilot Market:** Single township with 2,500–3,500 spaza retailers within 8–12km radius
- **Initial Target:** 400–600 retailers in Year 1 (15–20% penetration of addressable base)
- **Last-Mile Model:** Partner with 15–25 local van owners (2–3 tonne vans) for distribution
- **Revenue Streams:** Goods margin + Van owner commissions + Platform fees + Manufacturer ESD
- **Unit Economics:** 5–6% net margin in dense township (proven model before scaling)
- **Exit Strategy:** Prove pilot, then replicate across 3–5 townships nationally (Year 2–3)

**Investment Required (Pilot Phase):** R4–6M (18 months to breakeven in single township)

## 2. LAST-MILE DELIVERY MODEL: LOCAL VAN OWNER PARTNERSHIP

### The Problem with Traditional Delivery Models
- ❌ **Building own fleet:** Capital-intensive (R150k–R200k per van), complexity, maintenance risk
- ❌ **Hiring drivers:** Fixed cost burden, high turnover, limited accountability
- ❌ **Third-party logistics:** Lost margin (30–40%), less community integration, inflexible

### The TownshipLink Solution: Van Owner Partnership Model

**Partnership Structure:**
- Recruit 15–25 **local van owners** (2–3 tonne vans) already operating in Soweto
- Offer **commission-based delivery contracts** (no capital investment from TL)
- Van owners retain vehicle ownership, schedule flexibility, side income
- TL provides: retailer leads, order aggregation, payment guarantees, technology

### Van Owner Economics (Compelling Unit)

#### Van Owner Profile
- **Vehicle:** Toyota Quantum or Nissan NV350 (2–3 tonne capacity)
- **Current usage:** Often 50–70% utilized (school runs, ad-hoc freight, taxis)
- **Current daily income:** R300–R600/day (inconsistent)
- **Operating cost:** ~R800–R1,200/day (fuel, maintenance, insurance)
- **Motivation:** Formalize income stream, fill vehicle capacity, reduce fuel waste

#### Commission-Based Pricing Model

```
Example: Van Owner "Thabo" operates in Soweto West
- Service area: 5 retailers per route per day (dense township)
- Orders per retailer: 2x/week (Monday, Thursday)
- Weekly routes: 10 deliveries/day × 5 days = 50 deliveries/week
- Commission per delivery: R25–R35 (standard distance, 0–8km)

Weekly income:        50 deliveries × R30 avg. = R1,500/week
Monthly income:       R1,500 × 4 weeks = R6,000/month (GROSS)

Van operating cost:   R800/day × 5 days = R4,000/month
Net income:           R6,000 – R4,000 = R2,000/month
Annual income uplift: R24,000/year extra income (50% capacity utilization gain)
```

#### Why Van Owners Accept This Model
✅ **Stable income stream:** TL guarantees 40–50 consistent deliveries/week (no "waiting for freight")  
✅ **Vehicle already exists:** No capital investment required  
✅ **Fills empty capacity:** Uses otherwise-idle vehicle time  
✅ **Community credibility:** Trusted neighborhood face delivering from TL  
✅ **Flexible scheduling:** Can manage their own routes during off-peak hours  

### TownshipLink's Delivery Margin Capture

```
Retail order value:                     R4,000 (typical)
TL buys wholesale:                      R3,760 (cost of goods)
TL gross margin on goods:               R240 (6%)

Delivery cost to van owner:             -R30 to -R35
  (If 5 retailers per route = R150–R175 daily / 5 = R30–R35 per drop)

Net platform margin per order:          R205–R210 (5.1–5.25%)
```

### Van Owner Recruitment & Management

#### Recruitment Strategy (Month 1–2)
- **Identify:** Partner with local taxi associations, vehicle rental cooperatives, existing informal transport networks
- **Target profile:** Vehicle owners aged 30–55, 5+ years driving experience, registered with SARS, established in community
- **Incentive:** First 10 van owners get bonus R500/month for first 3 months (loyalty + referral incentive)
- **Training:** Weekly in-person training (order handling, customer service, payment reconciliation)

#### Recruitment Targets
- **Month 1:** 5–8 van owners (pilot routes)
- **Month 2–3:** 15–20 van owners (full Soweto coverage)
- **Month 4+:** 25–30 van owners (redundancy, peak capacity)

#### Performance Metrics & Churn Management
- **On-time delivery rate:** Target 98%+ (incentive bonus R200/month if maintained)
- **Customer satisfaction:** Quarterly retailer surveys
- **Churn expectation:** 10–15% per year (seasonal attrition, vehicle sales)
- **Retention incentive:** 1-year performance bonus of R1,500 (keep best drivers)

## 4. VAN OWNER OPERATIONAL PLAYBOOK

### Van Owner Recruitment & Onboarding (Week 1–8)

#### Week 1: Identify & Approach
- Partner with Soweto Taxi Association, vehicle rental cooperatives
- Target: Vehicle owners with 50–70% current utilization
- Initial pitch: "Earn R1,500–R3,000/month filling empty van space"
- Screening: Basic background check, vehicle inspection, SARS tax compliance

#### Week 2–3: Contract Signing & Training
- **Contract terms:**
  - R25–R35 per delivery (based on distance, zone)
  - Payment every Friday (weekly settlement)
  - 30-day trial period (either party can exit)
  - Liability insurance required (TL can help arrange)
  - Non-compete clause (don't poach our retailers)

- **Training topics (6 hours in-person):**
  - Order handling (fragile items, temperature control)
  - Route optimization (WhatsApp order feed)
  - Customer interaction (professionalism, payment collection)
  - Safety & security protocols
  - Payment reconciliation & reporting

#### Week 4: Soft Launch
- 3–5 pilot deliveries per van owner (supervised)
- Route testing: identify optimal delivery windows, traffic patterns
- Retailer introductions (meet Zone Captain, build relationship)

#### Week 5+: Full Operation
- 20–50 deliveries per week per van owner
- Weekly performance tracking
- Monthly bonus incentives (on-time %, customer satisfaction)

### Van Owner Performance Management

#### Weekly Metrics (Tracked via WhatsApp + Simple CRM)
- Deliveries completed
- On-time delivery % (within 30-min window)
- Customer complaints/issues
- Payment reconciliation accuracy

#### Monthly Incentive Structure
```
Base commission:           R25–R35 per delivery
On-time bonus:            +R200/month if 98%+ on-time rate
Customer satisfaction:    +R150/month if 4.5+ star rating (5-point scale)
Referral incentive:       +R500 per new van owner recruited & retained 3+ months
Loyalty bonus:            +R500 at 6-month mark; +R1,000 at 12-month mark

Example "Thabo" (top performer):
50 deliveries/week × R30 = R1,500 base
On-time bonus:            +R200
Satisfaction bonus:       +R150
1 referral active:        +R500
6-month loyalty:          +R500
Monthly total:            R2,850/month (+50% vs. base)
```

#### Churn Mitigation
- **Early warning signs:** Declining delivery count, missed collections, customer complaints
- **Intervention:** 1-on-1 review, address issues, offer support (maintenance assistance, better routes)
- **Exit process:** 2-week notice, settle outstanding payments same day
- **Replacement pipeline:** Always recruiting 2–3 backup van owners to fill gaps

### Quality Assurance & Accountability

#### Retailer Feedback Loop
- Weekly retailer satisfaction survey (via WhatsApp): "Delivery on time? Yes/No | Any issues?"
- Escalation path: Issue → Zone Captain → Hub Manager → Van Owner discussion
- Monthly van owner performance report (shared transparently)

#### Loss & Damage Protocol
- Van owner liable for delivery damage (up to R500/incident)
- TL covers manufacturer defects
- Clear photographic documentation at each step
- Dispute resolution within 48 hours
